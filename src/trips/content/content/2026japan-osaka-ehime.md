[Last time](/trips/content/2019jpn-twn) I rode around Japan, I learnt the hard way that in general, Japan's roads
aren't very cyclist-friendly. However, the Shimanami Kaido is a cycle route in Ehime set up specifically for cycling
tourists!

<!--more-->

I heard about the Shimanami Kaido on an Instagram reel, and as I was visiting Osaka for a conference (Supercomputing
Asia 2026), I thought it would be a good opportunity to stretch my legs and give the famous cycle route a go!
One of the reasons it was convenient for me on this trip is that there is an [overnight Orange Ferry](https://www.orange-ferry.co.jp/en/osaka.html)
which takes you directly to one end of the Shimanami Kaido in Ehime.

The Shimanami Kaido is a roughly 70km route between the towns of Imabari (on the Ehime side in the south) and
Onomichi (on the Hiroshima side in the north). It's become famous because it is a route that is specifically made for
cyclists, with ample cycling infrastructure including completely separated bike paths, plenty of bike shops and bike
rental options, and bike repair stations on the way. But the real draw is that the route is made up of a collection
of islands between Imabari and Onomichi, joined together by bridges with special bike lanes, providing both a safe ride
and gorgeous views of the Seto Inland Sea that the bridges cross.

I did this trip in winter (early Feb). I wouldn't recommend that time of year! It's quiet, but it's freezing and I got quite sick afterward 😷. Best times are apparently spring or autumn (March-May or September-November).

## Taking the Orange Ferry

The Orange Ferry goes between the [Nanko Passenger Terminal (in Osaka)](https://maps.app.goo.gl/hAHrepxnLa4ovcaR7) and
the [Toyo Port Ferry Terminal](https://maps.app.goo.gl/JYZ5qno7A6MsxjGU6). At the time of writing, the ferry departs at
10pm and arrives at 6am on either end. Thankfully, their [website](https://www.orange-ferry.co.jp/en/osaka.html) has
English and bookings can be made relatively easily. I was travelling solo, so went with the single room option with
breakfast. Together, this cost me ¥10,850 from Osaka to Ehime and ¥9,860 back to Osaka (both paid when I arrived at the
terminal in Osaka).

The Nanko passenger terminal can be reached by metro, and it's only a short and well-signed
walk from the station. To get from the Toyo port terminal to the start of the cycle (usually Imabari JR station), you
can take a bus. The bus service is lined up with the arrival and departure of the Orange Ferry and will usually say
that it's intended for ferry passengers. The bus fare is included with the ferry ticket as well, which is nice!

The Orange Ferry isn't the only way you can get to the route. If coming from Osaka, you can also take trains to
Onomichi station and start from that side instead. I chose to take the ferry mainly because it saved me 2 nights of
accommodation and it allowed me more time to ride, which I needed since I was doing the whole route in one day.

## Bike Rental

I decided to go with Giant, which has stores on both sides of the route. Similar to the ferry, the bike rentals can be
[booked in advance online](https://giant-store-rental.jp/en/rsv/) in English. They have a wide variety of options
suiting all riders - including e-bikes! I went with the Defy Advanced 2 since that seemed a good-value option.

{{< single img="/trips/img/trips-japan2026/giant.jpg" caption="Giant store at the Imabari station. Doesn't open until 9am, so expect a wait if you've arrived by ferry!" >}}

The only downside I had with this bike was that the saddle was too hard (even though I was wearing padded shorts)!
It might not have been a problem if I hadn't had a big backpack on.

## The route

A nice thing about the Shimanami Kaido is the wide variety of side-trip options. Apparently people usually take a few
days to do the entire route so they can explore the islands - usually staying in accommodation on the islands themselves. I was
on a bit of a schedule so I had opted to go out and back in 2 days - staying overnight in a capsule hotel in Onomichi.
Hence, I only followed the main route both ways. You do get plenty of nice views on the main route, but I can see how
some might want to take their time to soak in the islands' vibes.

Note that the longest bridge with arguably the best views is the Kurushima Kaikyo Bridge, which is the last bridge if
you're coming from Onomichi. Some might prefer starting at Onomichi as it saves the best for last.

On both days, I had lunch on Hakatajima Island - near [Hakata Beach](https://maps.app.goo.gl/HEWU6MzhAaywZGUz8).
The main reason was that the sandy beach, tourist shop, and restaurant seemed a welcoming spot to eat. Plus it was
about lunch time anyway! On the way there, I had a set with rice and eggs and udon. On the way back I had a smaller
ramen and gyoza set. I also picked up a cute Cycle Imabari T-shirt from the souvenir shop! On both days, I stopped at
a few 7-Elevens and FamilyMarts. I was cycling in winter (single-digit temps), so the warm drinks were very much needed!

If you follow the signs all the way to Onomichi, you'll be taken to a ferry terminal that exists solely to ferry people
(and vehicles) across to Onomichi proper. This is a perfectly fine option to go with since it only costs ¥110 with a
bike, plus riding the rest of the way would be mostly through suburban streets and isn't particularly scenic.

I stayed at a capsule hotel ([Station Inn the Urashima](https://maps.app.goo.gl/pDq6K3zFwSHmELsB9)), which sits directly
above the train station, with a few decent food options around for dinner. It was winter, so the capsule hotel was
mostly empty! If you do stay near the station, remember to park the bike in the nearby bike parking spot; otherwise
you might be in for a surprise when your bike has been moved!

The ride back was very much the same (which is a good thing, given that I got to enjoy the beautiful scenery a second
time), except that I was very much struggling with the saddle on the bike - my sit bones were suuuper sore! The lesson
here is that I'll probably bring my own saddle if I plan on doing long rides like this again in the future!

At the end of the ride, I rewarded myself with a fat Coco Ichibanya meal and a soak in the
[onsen near Imabari Station](https://maps.app.goo.gl/CUSEBDyCtRXboHV1A). But I did have to wait a while for the bus to
the Toyo Ferry terminal.

Would love to do this again during warmer weather!
