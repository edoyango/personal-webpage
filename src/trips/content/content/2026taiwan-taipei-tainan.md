Taiwan is a unique fusion of cultures and terrains, making a great combination for my wife's and my interests. It sits
directly on top of a tectonic plate line, meaning despite its small size, it possesses the tallest mountain peak in East
Asia - Jade Mountain (玉山). After already [cycling around Taiwan's circumference](/trips/content/2019jpn-twn/), I was
keen to summit Jade Mountain. My wife, on the other hand, is not as much of an avid hiker. She was more keen on
exploring the night markets and enjoying the vibrant food culture.

# Taipei

Taipei is where we flew into and we stayed at Simple + Hotel - near Nanjing Fuxing metro station. We chose this spot
because it was pretty good value and close to plenty of food options and public transport.

As part of this trip, we were spending time with one of my Dad's former PhD students, Chandler. He was kind enough to
take us to visit his hometown in Yilan - about an hour's drive away from Taipei. He took us to the Mr. Brown Cafe on the
hillside in Yilan, placed in a beautiful castle-like building and had a stunning view of the ocean. Well worth a visit
if you're in the area. He also took us to the Lanyang Museum - a museum about Yilan's history and worth a look just for
the interesting architecture inspired by coastal rock formations.

# Alishan

Alishan is a region in central Taiwan known for its tea, mountain views, and forests. To get there, we took the HSR to
Chiayi (about 1.5h, 970TWD pp), and took the 7229 bus up the mountain (about 3h, 283TWD pp). Both of these tickets we
[booked in advance](https://www.taiwanobsessed.com/alishan-bus-ticket-online/), but it turned out the bus up to the
Alishan recreation area had plenty of seats, so paying for tickets on the day would've been fine. The main advantage of
paying on arrival is that it lets you make stops on the way, to visit tea farms or stop at the Fengqihu old street.
Hiring a private driver could've also been an option, but we decided it wasn't worth it given our schedule.

We spent a total of 2 nights in the Alishan recreational area and so we spent the single whole day exploring the many
trails in the area. My highlight was walking along the Shuishan Healing Trail (水山療癒步道) which follows a former logging
transport rail and passes the ancient Shuishan Giant Tree before circling back to Zhaoping Station.

# Jade Mountain

## Logistics

The Jade Mountain hike was the most logistically difficult part of the trip. The Main Peak trail sits within Yushan
National Park's ecological protection area, so entering it at all - even as a single-day hike with no overnight stay -
requires a National Park Entry Permit and a Police Mountain Entry Permit (both free). Most people, myself included, do
it as an overnight instead: hike up to Paiyun Lodge, stay the night, then do the summit in the morning to catch the
sunrise before hiking back down. That means booking a bed on top of the entry permits, and that's the part with
genuinely limited, and fought-over, spots.

To get a spot at Paiyun Lodge for the evening of 24th September, I submitted an application on 11th June (over 3 months
in advance). All bookings go through the [Hike Smart Taiwan Service](https://hike.taiwan.gov.tw/en/web_index.aspx), but
which pathway you use depends on your passport:

- **Foreigners**: 24 beds a night reserved for foreign passport holders (Sunday-Thursday, excluding public holidays),
  first-come-first-served from 4 months to 35 days before your stay date. This is the pathway I used. Select "Paiyun
  Lodge Advanced Application", not "Standard Application", or you'll land in the general lottery instead.
- **General/Taiwanese public**: everyone else goes into the lottery via "Standard Application". Applications open 60
  days and close 30 days before the stay date, with the draw held shortly after.

You also have the option of booking food and sleeping bags at Paiyun Lodge in advance (via the [third-party
link](https://yushansky.com.tw/ordering/)). We went with this option as we didn't want to use up our luggage space. It
cost us 1050TWD each for dinner, (early) breakfast, and brunch, plus the sleeping bag.

Getting to the start of the hike is another logistical burden. There is a bus - route 6739 (Sun Moon Lake ↔ Alishan
line) - that goes there from Alishan station, getting off at the Shangdongpu stop near the trailhead. But it's
infrequent (twice a day) and at times that aren't particularly convenient: only 13:00 and 14:30 from Alishan station on
weekdays (13:00 and 14:00 on weekends/holidays), and only 10:38 and 12:08 from the Shangdongpu stop back to Alishan on
weekdays (10:38 and 11:08 on weekends/holidays). We instead opted to hire a private driver, arranged through our hotel
(Dafong Hotel). It cost us 4000TWD for the round trip to/from the Shangdongpu parking lot and took around 25 mins from
the hotel.

From the Shangdongpu parking lot, you walk a few minutes to Paiyun Mountaineering Centre, where you check in and pay for
the lodge stay (if you're booking as a foreigner). From there, you have the option of taking a 150TWD pp shuttle (comes
approx. every 15 mins) to the Tataka Trailhead.

## The hike

The hike to Paiyun Lodge is about 9.5km long. Online sources and the trail markers say the trail is 8.5km but my Garmin
says otherwise haha. The net elevation gain is around 860m gross, and 760m net. There's a good chance to see wildlife -
we saw a couple of Formosan Sambar Deer on the way up, and Yellow-throated Marten on the way down. The views going up
are gorgeous. We had clear skies in the morning and we could watch the clouds progressively come up the valley as we
progressed up the mountain. Worth mentioning is that you can do the west peak which forks from the path up about half
way, and is apparently a 2h return.

Including breaks, it took us around 4h from the Tataka Trailhead to Paiyun Lodge, which meant a lot of free time in the
afternoon. Dinner was quite early - at 4:30pm which was a cooked meal of beef, chicken, or pork. I got chicken cooked
with the classic golden curry - also with sides. We were in bed around 6pm. Sleeping in the bunks was quite tough - a
couple of very loud snorers and someone who groaned every 10 mins or so, seemingly frustrated at the snorers but
nevertheless contributing to everyone else's suffering.

Despite the awful sleep, I woke up early at 1:30am to get ready for the summit. I didn't get up at this time by choice -
the people getting up this early woke me up, so I figured I might as well. I also heard the steepness meant a long hike,
and I wanted to make sure I didn't miss out on the sunrise. I left Paiyun Lodge at 3am and arrived at the summit around
4am and learnt the hard way that it's good to pack some extra layers if you're going to sit around and wait for the
sunrise - especially if you're 1.5h early. If you're going to go early, it might be nice to do the north peak as well -
the fork being maybe 1/3 of the way up to the main peak. If going to the main peak, the hike is around 2.3km with ~520m
elevation gain (net and gross). The summit gets pretty crowded, so I found it worth going beyond the peak a little bit
as it had clear spots to take some longer-exposure shots. Once the sun has risen, be prepared to wait in line for a shot
with the summit marker!

# Tainan

After a long bus ride down the mountain and a 15-minute HSR ride to Tainan, we arrived in Tainan. Tainan had a much more
artistic vibe to it with modern art museums celebrating local artists and shopping streets with lanterns set up for
Mid-Autumn Festival. I had a go at looking for the city's official bird - the Pheasant-tailed Jacana, but it was at the
tail-end of the breeding season and there was only one Jacana with 2 chicks at the reserve and that Jacana had already
lost its yellow plumage and long tail.

{{< trio img1="/trips/img/trips-taiwan2026/jacana.jpeg" caption1="Last Pheasant-tailed Jacana father taking care of his chicks at the reserve" img2="/trips/img/trips-taiwan2026/tainan-art-museum.jpg" caption2="Amusing piece at the Tainan Art Museum" img3="/trips/img/trips-taiwan2026/tainan-shennong.jpg" caption3="Walking down Shennong Street" >}}

I highly recommend attending the Tainan National History Museum. It was an unexpectedly thorough and comprehensive
museum with a permanent exhibit that mostly details its history of being occupied by the Dutch, Spanish, Han Chinese,
Japanese, and KMT Chinese again. Quite out of the way, but worth the visit if you're even a little bit interested in
history.

{{< duo img1="/trips/img/trips-taiwan2026/museum1.jpg" caption1="The Taiwan history permanent exhibit" img2="/trips/img/trips-taiwan2026/museum2.jpg" caption2="Section of the Taiwan history museum discussing Qing dynasty Chinese immigration into Taiwan" >}}

But in the end, visiting Tainan was more about food. So we enjoyed many beef soups, guabaos, and tofu puddings! Our
favourite beef soup is from Alian Beef Soup (阿蓮牛肉湯), which had a very long line and was popular with tourists and
locals alike. Guohua St turned out to be a nice place to visit because it had good food options (guabaos in
particular) and the Asakusa Shopping Centre - also good for souvenirs and food!
